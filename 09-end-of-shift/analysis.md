Prima facie, there's nothing wrong with Goyal's claim. It's reasonable to
believe that most Indians are still at their desk at 5 pm, and most Indian do
work on weekends. In fact, we could simply replace the generalizing phrases like
"nobody in India" with "very few people in India", and the statement could even
be taken literally. After all, even in the most emancipated work cultures, you
can barely expect people to follow the strict 9-to-5 schedule.

However, it is the _spirit_ of the statement that is under scrutiny here. What
does the working class in India do in the evenings and on weekends? Who,
precisely, are the people who work late evenings, and how do they differ from
people who don't? To anwser these questions, we turn (once again[^90hr]) to
NSO's Time Use Survey of 2024. The survey itself is comprehensive, and paints a
detailed picture of an average day in the life of practically every Indian. But
we limit ourselves only to salaried[^regularwagetoo] respodents—mostly to keep things as
relatable as possible—I'm salaried, and you are most likely salaried, too. This
cohort is then divided into five mutually exclusive groups based on their
industrial sector[^nco]:

1. Manufacturing and construction
2. Trade, transport and hospitality
3. Knowledge services (IT, media, finance, professional & scientific services,
   etc)
4. Education, health and administration (public and private)
5. Other industries (agriculture, real estate, utilities, etc)
